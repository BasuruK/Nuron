# Reconciliation: prd.md vs brief.md

## Gaps (things in brief.md not adequately captured in prd.md)

- **The "moat" framing is entirely absent.** Brief's "What Makes This Different" section explicitly names pillar #1 ("Continuity across human turnover") as **"the moat"** and structures all four pillars around defensibility. The PRD's Vision (§1) restates the functional behavior (decisions as first-class, lineage at write time) but drops the word "moat" and the explicit ranking ("in order of how much they matter") entirely. A reader of the PRD alone would not know this is the company's core competitive thesis, not just a feature.

- **Competitive differentiation reasoning (Mem0 / Cognee / Graphiti) is completely dropped.** Brief pillar #2 explains supersession-at-write-time is explicitly differentiated from Mem0 (retrieval-time ranking, not graph invalidation) and Cognee (no explicit decision entity), and is closest to but distinct from Graphiti's bi-temporal `valid_at`/`invalid_at` model. Pillar #3 explains none of the three competitors run an external broker for their async pipeline (Mem0 OSS synchronous, Cognee `run_in_background`, Graphiti per-`group_id` in-memory `asyncio.Queue`s lost on restart) — this is the whole justification for why RabbitMQ-as-default is a "durability story," not just an implementation choice. Pillar #4 names Graphiti's PostHog telemetry-on-by-default and Mem0/Cognee's analytics hooks as the reason "no telemetry by default" is a procurement-friendly differentiator. None of this competitive grounding survives into the PRD's FR-4, FR-6, or NFR sections — those requirements now read as arbitrary engineering choices rather than moat-reinforcing decisions.

- **"What is not a moat" section is dropped.** Brief explicitly states embeddings, LLM choice, Graph RAG technique, and storage backend are commodity and not defensible — this is a scope-protection statement (don't over-invest here) that has no equivalent anywhere in the PRD.

- **Mental model / three-axes framing (domains × flows × people × systems) and its diagram are missing.** The brief's ASCII diagram and the framing that "knowledge is fragmented by structure, not just by content volume" across domains, flows, people, and systems is a core explanatory device for *why* the problem is hard. The PRD's Problem-equivalent content (folded into §1 Vision and §2 JTBD) never reproduces this model or diagram, so the "why fragmentation happens" reasoning is lost, only the personas survive.

- **"Operational" and "people-handling" knowledge categories are narrowed away.** Brief is explicit that in-scope knowledge includes not just product/code documentation but **operational knowledge** (release processes, on-call rotations, vendor relationships) and **people-handling knowledge** (how decisions about teams, hires, and reorganisations were reached) — and states deliberately that "the brain does not distinguish product/operational/people-handling knowledge at the data-model level." PRD §1 Vision and the Glossary's "Entity / Knowledge Node" definition only mention "product documentation, code documentation, or tacit business-flow knowledge" — operational and people-handling knowledge are never named. This narrows the apparent scope of what the Entity/Knowledge Node type is meant to cover, even though no FR explicitly excludes it.

- **The three "visible symptoms" of decision loss are not carried forward as explicit statements.** Brief names three symptoms plainly: (1) people touch non-specified/adjacent areas just to learn, (2) cross-functional knowledge lives in people not systems, (3) operational/people-handling knowledge is treated as out of scope by competing tools. The PRD's JTBD (§2.1) and journeys (§2.3) gesture at symptom #2 via Kian's journey but don't name symptoms #1 or #3 anywhere, so the "cost of the gap" argument is thinner in the PRD.

- **The 2–3 year Vision section's explicit outlook and "switching cost is the graph itself" argument are dropped.** Brief's dedicated "Vision" section makes two concrete claims about the 2–3 year outlook (departures stop being catastrophic; the brain compounds) and ends with an explicit business argument: "Customers who have run Nuron for two years will not switch off it; the switching cost is the graph itself." The PRD's §1 Vision is retitled but functions more like an expanded executive summary — the explicit multi-year time horizon and the switching-cost/retention argument do not appear anywhere in the PRD.

- **"Quality bar" test criterion for the embedding model floor is dropped.** Brief's embedding floor subsection includes an explicit, testable bar: "the embedding model must produce semantically meaningful similarity on enterprise-shaped text... We will not ship an embedding model that fails this bar on a representative seed corpus." PRD §5.2 keeps the parameter/dimension floor and the OpenAI-class default but drops this qualitative acceptance criterion entirely — it's a testable requirement (akin to the other SM-* metrics) that has no home in the PRD's Success Metrics section.

- **The rationale for touched-subtree-only curation (avoiding "unbounded write amplification") is lost.** Brief's Solution section explains *why* the curator only touches changed subtrees — "so the graph stays current without unbounded write amplification." PRD's FR-7 states the behavior and its performance budget but never states the underlying problem being avoided, which matters if Architecture later needs to justify the Merkle-indexing hypothesis (Q-E) against a simpler alternative.

- **Frontend Stack Decision's rationale and comparison table are not referenced at all.** The brief spends a large section justifying Svelte-over-Laravel-served-React and Bits-UI-over-shadcn-svelte with a full dimension-by-dimension comparison table and explicit "when to revisit toward shadcn-svelte" criteria. The PRD states the decided stack (`nuron-web` = SvelteKit + Bits UI) as a bare fact in §1, Glossary, and §5.3, with zero rationale carried forward. If a future contributor asks "why Bits UI, not shadcn-svelte," the PRD alone gives no answer — only the brief does.

- **The "pipeline is the spine, Markdown is the proof" framing and its explicit separability claim are absent.** Brief's Boundary Clarifications state this metaphor and explicitly note the pipeline can be shipped/validated against Markdown seed data before any connector work is needed. The PRD's §5.5 Rollout & Phasing captures the v1/v1.1 split functionally but never states this as a deliberate staging principle.

- **Scale/urgency justification for the problem ("hundreds of emails, thousands of Confluence pages, thousands of changes per week") is not carried into the PRD.** This is narrative texture supporting why the problem is structural at scale; the PRD has no equivalent framing.

## Contradictions (unintentional discrepancies)

- No numeric or scope contradictions were found. Every quantitative claim that appears in both documents matches: the embedding-model floor (≥600M-parameter class, ≥1024-dim, `text-embedding-3-large`/3072-dim default), the curator performance budget (10k-node graph, 1% touched subtrees, <10 minutes), the v1/v1.1/v2 scope boundaries (Confluence/Jira/forum connectors, write-back, OIDC/SSO, multi-tenant), the resolved Open Questions (Q-B, Q-C, Q-D, Q-F, Q-I are correctly omitted from the PRD's still-open list since brief.md already resolved them), and the Frontend Stack Decision (Bits UI locked) are all consistent between the two documents.
- The one apparent framework discrepancy (brief says LangGraph throughout; PRD says LlamaIndex) is the explicitly intentional, already-tracked correction per the instructions — not flagged here.
